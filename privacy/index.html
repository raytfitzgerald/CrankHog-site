Privacy policy

Your app data stays on your device.

Updated 19 November 2026. Covers the Mac app, the iPhone app, the web app and this website.

What stays on your device

Your rides, other activities, workouts, training plans, calendar, routes, journeys, FTP history, profile (name, photo, weight, heart-rate zones, threshold paces) and settings are stored only on your Mac, iPhone or browser. Every ride is also saved as a FIT file on the same device. The CrankHog apps have no accounts, no servers that hold your ride data, no analytics, no tracking and no ads. Website download counts are described separately below.

Bluetooth

CrankHog uses Bluetooth only to connect to your smart trainer, heart-rate strap and other cycling sensors, so it can set the resistance and record your ride. Nothing about your devices leaves your device.

Camera and photos

The iPhone app uses the camera only if you choose to take a photo for your rider or for a coach you make, and the photo library only if you pick one. The photo is shrunk to a small thumbnail and stays on your device with the rest of your profile. It is never sent to an AI provider.

Things you choose to connect

Nothing is sent anywhere unless you set it up yourself:

  • Strava (Mac app): when you connect your account, finished rides are uploaded to it. CrankHog never reads your Strava data.
  • intervals.icu (Mac app): when you connect your account, finished rides are uploaded to it. If you ask, CrankHog also brings in the workouts you plan there and, if you turn it on, your completed activities, and sends the planned runs and swims you choose to it.
  • Google Calendar (Mac app): if you sign in with Google, CrankHog makes a calendar named CrankHog in your Google account and keeps your planned sessions in it, from 30 days ago onward: each one's name, day and a description (sport, length, distance, effort, load, notes and steps). It can only reach the calendar it made, reading that calendar's events to keep them matching your plan. It can't see or change your other calendars or events. Signing out revokes its access. Google's privacy policy applies.
  • AI coaching (Mac app): if you add an API key for Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI or use a local Ollama, the coach chat sends that provider what you keep in CrankHog, so it can answer you and propose changes: your rides, runs, swims and other activities (including those from intervals.icu), your training plans, calendar, races and days off, and your training numbers, such as FTP, weight, heart-rate zones and threshold paces. A ride debrief sends that ride, and the fitness overview your fitness numbers. If you make your own coach, its name, description and master prompt go too, and so do the notes you write for your coaches. If you import a plan document with AI, its text is sent on its own, only after you confirm. Your name, your photos and anything from Strava are never sent. Their own privacy policies apply.
  • Claude Desktop and Claude Code (Mac app): if you turn on Claude in Settings → AI, those Claude apps on your Mac can read what the coach chat gets (see AI coaching) and, if you allow it, change your calendar, workouts and plans. What Claude reads goes to Anthropic under your own Claude account, and Anthropic's privacy policy applies. Only apps running on your Mac under your account can reach CrankHog. Your name, your photos and anything from Strava are never sent.
  • Ride with others (Mac app, beta): if you turn it on, CrankHog connects to an OpenBikeNet server: openbikenet.cloud unless you name another, run by the OpenBikeNet project, not by CrankHog. It tells the server which shared track you're looking at, and while you ride one it sends, once a second, the name you chose for other riders, your lap, distance and speed, your power and cadence unless you turn them off, and your heart rate only if you turn it on. Everyone on that track sees them, and which app you use. The server also sees your IP address. Nothing is sent while it's off.
  • Bug reports: the report form opens a GitHub issue in your browser with the details you can see and edit. Nothing is sent unless you submit it on GitHub.

Any keys and tokens for these are kept in your Mac's keychain.

Maps

Journey maps are drawn from road data that ships inside the app (© OpenStreetMap contributors). The real map and the 3D view on a journey (Mac app) load map tiles from OpenFreeMap and terrain from the AWS Terrain Tiles dataset, so those services see your IP address and which part of the world is on screen. The real map is used for CrankHog's famous journeys to start with. Routes you import stay on the drawn map unless you choose otherwise in Settings → Journeys, where you can also turn the real map off. If you turn on "Map on Strava", the virtual route is written into that ride's FIT file and uploaded with it.

Children

CrankHog isn't aimed at children. The apps collect no data from anyone, of any age.

Website download counts

This website uses GoatCounter to count clicks on the Mac download buttons. These are download attempts, not completed downloads or installs. This tracking does not run inside the apps.

Every recorded click is counted, including repeat clicks. This is not a count of unique people. GoatCounter processes the IP address and browser User-Agent that accompany network requests. It does not store raw IP addresses or full User-Agent strings in its database.

No visitor identifier is saved in cookies or browser storage. This integration sends fixed event names rather than page query strings or referring URLs, and does not send automatic pageviews. Optional browser, device, language and location statistics, visitor sessions, and individual pageview storage are disabled in our GoatCounter settings. Visitors with Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track enabled are skipped.
